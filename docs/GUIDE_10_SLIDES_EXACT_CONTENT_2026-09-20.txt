SLIDE 1 - Literature survey and research gap
Title: Polarimetric ISAR component detection: published benchmark
Body:
  - Pol-ISARSpaceTarget-1.0 supplies four complex polarization channels and
    oriented component annotations for fine-grained space-target recognition.
  - The published comparison covers O-FCOS, PSC, O-RTMDet, O-ATSS, S2A-Net
    and O-Faster R-CNN over 12 viewing-condition protocols.
  - O-RTMDet has the best reported mean mAP50: 68.28% across those conditions.
  - Research question: can phase-aware fusion improve detection on unseen views?
Figure: translated_paper_method_map50.png
Caption: Published mean mAP50 across 12 viewing conditions; NOT our result.
Source: Dataset paper DOI 10.12466/xhcl.2025.08.013 and supplied English translation.

SLIDE 2 - Dataset
Title: Pol-ISARSpaceTarget-1.0: inputs, labels and split
Body:
  - 1,638 ISAR frames; six target identities; nine component classes.
  - Four complex channels per frame: HH, HV, VH and VV; image size 256 x 256.
  - 16,577 oriented component instances in the dataset report.
  - Clean split: 1,314 train / 162 validation / 162 test frames.
Figure: guide_slide02_polarizations_and_real_obbs.png
Caption: One real Capella frame in four polarizations, with its VV component labels.
Source: Pol-ISAR dataset TIFFImages and ImageSets/labelTxt; project dataset audit.

SLIDE 3 - Classification using a conventional CNN
Title: Magnitude-only ResNet18 baseline
Body:
  - Input: four polarization magnitude images; output: six target classes.
  - ResNet18 was trained for 15 epochs.
  - Held-out test accuracy: 41.36%; macro-F1: 38.05%.
  - Training approached 99% accuracy, but validation was unstable: this run
    did not generalize well. It was not a tuned, protocol-matched comparison.

SLIDE 4 - Classification using a complex CNN
Title: Complex CNN with magnitude-phase feature fusion
Body:
  - Complex HH/HV/VH/VV data are processed by complex convolutions.
  - A fully connected feature vector is combined with magnitude/phase features
    for six-class classification; this slide is classification-only.
  - Train-statistics-normalized held-out test: 97.53% accuracy,
    97.49% macro-F1, 97.74% macro precision.
  - An optional attention overlay comes from an earlier exploratory
    cross-attention model, not the final clean ComplexCNN checkpoint.

SLIDE 5 - K-fold and held-out results
Title: Five-fold validation exposes variation hidden by one split
Body:
  - Stratified five-fold evaluation on train + validation; eight epochs/fold.
  - Fold accuracies (%): 99.66, 97.97, 97.97, 99.32, 96.61.
  - Mean accuracy: 98.31 +/- 1.09 percentage points.
  - Mean macro-F1: 98.29 +/- 1.11 percentage points.
  - Held-out test: 158/162 correct (97.53%); four errors appear in the matrix.

SLIDE 6 - Normalization
Title: Fit normalization on training samples only
Body:
  - Real and imaginary channel mean/std were computed on each fold's training
    partition, then reused unchanged on that fold's validation partition.
  - No standardization: 90.11 +/- 5.52% mean validation accuracy.
  - Fold-training-only standardization: 98.31 +/- 1.09%.
  - Selected learning rate: 3 x 10^-4, based on the training/validation sweep.
  - Test-set statistics did not participate in fitting normalization.
Figure: classification_kfold_normalization_comparison.png
Caption: Accuracy across the same five fold positions with and without standardization.
Source: POLISAR_ComplexCNN_Classification_CLEAN (1) and (2).ipynb.

SLIDE 7 - Object detection
Title: Oriented component detection: model capacity and fusion baselines
Body:
  - YOLO11n-OBB, VV: mAP50 82.1%; mAP50-95 58.2%.
  - YOLO11n-OBB, fixed Pauli fusion: 78.3%; 57.3%.
  - YOLO11n-OBB, learned fusion: 74.3%; 51.8%.
  - At 100 epochs on VV, YOLO11s reached 85.28% / 62.90% and YOLO11m
    reached 87.05% / 65.24% (mAP50 / mAP50-95).
  - In a separate completed 100-epoch VV run, YOLO26s reached 85.90% / 65.01%
    and YOLO26m reached 88.02% / 66.70% (mAP50 / mAP50-95).
  - These are reported test values from separate executed runs; a matched
    evaluation under one protocol is the next comparison.

SLIDE 8 - Custom architecture and physics-informed rules
Title: Completed baselines and proposed phase-aware fusion
Body:
  - Completed: 100-epoch VV-only YOLO11s/m and YOLO26s/m detector runs;
    YOLO26m has the highest reported result, 88.02% mAP50 / 66.70% mAP50-95.
  - Completed: an earlier 50-epoch YOLO11n comparison of VV, fixed Pauli and
    learned fusion; VV was best in that comparison (82.1% mAP50).
  - Proposed model input: complex scattering channels HH, HV, VH and VV.
  - Physics-informed features: log magnitude and relative phase
    delta_phi_p = arg(S_p * conjugate(S_VV)); encode phase as sin/cos.
  - Conclusion: higher-capacity VV baselines are strong, while the initial
    fusion variants did not improve over VV. A relative-phase-aware fusion
    detector remains a testable hypothesis, not a demonstrated improvement.
Figure: guide_slide08_proposed_physics_fusion_architecture.png
Caption: Proposed relative-phase design; arrows show data flow, not trained
         performance. Existing detector results are from separate runs.
Source: Project proposal; inspired by cross-view gap in translated paper.
